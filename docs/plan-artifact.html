02 / Tone

Never comment on the joke

A rooster is ungovernable. A goblin is a middle manager. A demon is mid-level and wants you to know it isn't even the interesting one. The game reports all of this in the same flat voice it uses for damage numbers.

Over-the-top comes from the world behaving this way sincerely, never from nudging the player. All flavour text is data — one word-bank file plus per-monster lines — so the writing can be revised without touching Go.

The hiring board is where the voice does most of its work now. "I work cheap because the alternative is going home." "Technically I'm still under contract to a previous employer. Technically he's dead." Nobody involved acknowledges that any of it is unusual.

04 / Status

Built, and deliberately not built

  • BuiltA 160×120 generated continent — noise-driven elevation, moisture and temperature, island falloff, rivers that run downhill to the sea, roads connecting every settlement to the capital
  • Built~40 locations per world across eleven kinds, each regenerating its own interior from its seed rather than being stored
  • BuiltTurn-based combat: initiative rolled per party member, targeting on both sides of the field, techniques, items, defending, fleeing
  • Built62 monsters across nine biomes, each with its own attack verbs, taunts, death lines and drop table — and, for fourteen of them, something they leave in you
  • BuiltA party — up to two hirelings, hired outside inns, never commanded. They run the policy the balance simulator plays, take hits that would otherwise be yours, and level on their own curve
  • BuiltHirelings who are not entirely people — six lineages, each a stat trade-off with a discount attached, and each carrying one technique no hero of any class or level can learn
  • BuiltOutfitting the company — the shop counter turns to whoever you point it at, and supplies bought for a hireling go in their pack and are drunk without asking
  • BuiltStatus effects — one timed list per combatant. Poison and burning tick, weakness and blessings net against each other, a stun costs a turn
  • BuiltFour equipment slots and an authored affix table, banded by tier, found in chests rather than sold — and offered rather than equipped, because every affix is a trade
  • BuiltGetting back up — a fallen companion is out of the fight, not dead; a fallen hero is carried to the nearest town by whoever is still standing, for a large share of the purse
  • BuiltQuests — fetch, cull, delve and deliver, generated from nearby locations and drop tables, naming only things the generator has checked exist
  • BuiltSave and load — three slots, plus a committed set of fixtures that double as a regression net and as playtest starting points
  • BuiltBalance — a simulator that plays the game's own code, and the tuning passes it has justified four times now
  • BuiltSound, blended terrain, scenery, interior clutter, 229 icons, and an asset pipeline over a 16.7 GB bundle that never blocks the build
  • LaterCompanion and NPC backstories — the ordered-thread mechanism the generated errands deliberately are not
  • LaterAlternative progression arcs, so that "on curve" stops being a single correct build
05 / Structure

Five decisions in the architecture

A scene stack, not a state enum

A shop, a battle, a message box and the map each push themselves over whatever was underneath and pop back. No screen needs to know who called it, and an overlay draws the scene beneath it — so a battle gets a dimmed backdrop of the place it started.

Every generator takes an RNG, never a global

RNG.Fork(label, salt) derives child streams deterministically. That is what lets a location rebuild its own interior from its seed on every visit instead of being stored, and what makes a seed reproduce the continent, the towns, and the jokes.

go run ./cmd/slycrel -seed 1994   # the same world, every time

Missing art never blocks the build

Any asset key that will not resolve renders as a generated placeholder — dithered pixel-art tiles for terrain, a marker for anything else. The manifest points either at curated files or straight into the extracted bundle, so curation is incremental and reversible, and -audit reports exactly what is still standing in.

The simulator plays the game's own code

internal/rules is pure — no I/O, no globals, the RNG passed in — so the balance report exercises the real arithmetic rather than a copy of it. Two rules follow. A mechanic the simulator cannot see is a mechanic the balance pass is lying about, which is why it learned to apply and tick poison the day monsters started inflicting it. And the "properly equipped" assumption is load-bearing: adding two equipment slots moved the entire report without a single stat changing, purely by making that assumption richer.

Domain logic stays out of the scene layer

Ebitengine opens a window at package init, so anything importing it cannot run without a display — and on this machine the display drops for twenty minutes at a time. The roster and the marching order live in internal/party, the tile walker in internal/core, the maths in internal/rules. Eighty-odd assertions run anywhere; thirteen need a window. The save fixtures are the same idea in data form: a committed run in a state that is otherwise a chore to reach, checked against a freshly generated continent without drawing a pixel.

06 / Next

Roadmap

  1. Make it feel like a game (done)

    • Save and load — the seed plus what the player changed, a few kilobytes of readable JSON
    • Audio — 33 cues over 81 files, four ambience beds, and a retired magician who comments on your victories
    • Real terrain tiles — quarter-tile corner autotiling over the bundle's ground textures
    • Icons everywhere — on every item, weapon, suit of armour and technique
    • Quests — generated from the world rather than written against it
    • A party — hired, never commanded, and the reason a fallen hero gets carried to town instead of losing the run

  2. Depth (status effects and equipment done)

    • Status effects — one timed list per combatant, replacing four ad-hoc mechanisms the battle screen had grown
    • Equipment slots and affixes — four slots, authored suffixes, every one a trade
    • Companion backstories — a two- or three-step thread per hireling, resolved at a place the world already generated
    • NPC backstories — the same shape for the townspeople who currently have one line each

  3. A world that reacts

    • Alternative progression arcs — two or three builds that are each viable rather than one that is correct, and a balance column for each
    • Day, night and weather, changing encounter tables and who is out
    • Reputation — fame, honour, faith and shame exist on the character and currently do almost nothing
    • A generated main thread stringing five or six locations into something with an ending

  4. Polish

    • Curated interface art from the 4,488-file GUI kit, replacing the procedural panels
    • Title art, transitions, particles from the VFX pack
    • Balance simulation at volume: 10,000 headless fights per level band
07 / Open

Still undecided

How big should the world be?
160×120 crosses on foot in a couple of minutes. Larger needs fast travel; smaller needs denser locations.
Is there more than one way to be correctly levelled?
Right now, no — and the code says so out loud: one function decides what "properly equipped" means, and the balance report measures that build alone. Two or three arcs, each viable, would turn "is this balanced" into "is each of these playable". The cheap half is a column per archetype; the expensive half is content with enough spread to support them.
Is anyone else playing?
The original was a door game, and the Go recreation was built session-per-user against a shared store. Worth settling before the save format hardens further — it is already on its second version.

Settled since the last revision: what happens when you die. Fall with somebody still standing and the company carries you to the nearest town for a large share of the purse and a point of Shame. Fall alone and it is still the end — so permadeath survives for anyone playing alone, and the hirelings are the thing that buys it off.
